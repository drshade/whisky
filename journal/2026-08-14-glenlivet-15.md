# Glenlivet 15 French Oak Reserve — 2026-08-14

Sampled out on holiday, not a proper tasting. Very hot, so I drank half of it plain and
put an ice block in for the rest. Initial thoughts only.

## Overall
Nice, but very plain and smooth — not much character. Really inoffensive, and also
nothing particularly interesting. Maybe that's the point.

**Confidence: Low.** Three confounds at once — ice mutes aromatics, the heat, and a
casual setting rather than a considered pour.

## Why it probably read that way

Beyond the setting, the bottle itself is built for broad appeal rather than character:

- **40% ABV** — the legal minimum, and I've been drinking 46%+ all week.
- **Chill-filtered** — strips the fatty acids and esters that carry body and mouthfeel,
  in exchange for not clouding over ice.
- **Caramel colouring (E150)** for visual consistency across batches.
- Only *a portion* gets the French Limousin oak finish; the rest is plain ex-bourbon.

Whiskybase community has it at **81.7 from 785 ratings** — low for a 15-year-old single
malt from a major distillery, so the crowd broadly agrees.

## Fits the existing profile
This lands almost exactly where `notes/preferences.md` already predicted: *"Not drawn to
the big crowd-pleaser brands' entry expressions (Glenfiddich / Glenlivet / Glenmorangie
12s) — the bright/gentle/simple style I find dull."* Second data point for that note,
now at 15 years rather than 12.

Worth remembering the same file's caveat though: **judge by expression, not brand.**
Glenlivet's Nàdurra line is cask strength and non-chill-filtered — a completely different
proposition, and the Nàdurra Oloroso is already on my wishlist at ~€70.
