# Three Ships 6 Chenin — 2026-08-11

Third dram of the night, after the Springbank 10 and Kilkerran 12 head-to-head. Opened
deliberately to follow the white-wine thread from the Campbeltowns — this one is an
actual white wine cask finish (4yr American oak, then 2yr Chenin Blanc), where the
Campbeltown "Sauvignon Blanc" note was spirit-derived rather than from any wine cask.

**Confidence: Low.** A few drams in by this point. Worth a sober revisit.

## The R15 footnote

Last time I drank Three Ships was as a student in Grahamstown, around 1997–98, when it
was a R15 bottle of absolute cheapest garbage. We had a running joke about the 1-2-3s —
**First Watch, Two Keys, Three Ships** — the cheap spirits ladder, ordered by price.
Student drinking was a serious pursuit and cheaper was better.

Nearly thirty years later the same name is on a bottle I'd call genuinely impressive.
The distillery turned around under **Andy Watts** at James Sedgwick — an Englishman who
came to South Africa to play cricket and stayed to run the place. The same distillery now
makes the Bain's 15yr Founders Collection, which sits at 89 in this catalogue.

From the bottom rung of the 1-2-3 ladder to this. Worth recording.

## Overall
Really good, and completely different to the Campbeltowns — sweet and rich where they
were mineral and dry. Just short of the top tier on complexity, hence 83.

Two things worth carrying forward:

**It reads sherried, but there is no sherry in it.** The dark berry richness is coming
from the Chenin cask and the American oak. That matters: the sherried-style richness I
chase can also be reached via white wine cask finishes, which are cheaper and far less
hunted than good sherry-cask bottles. My wishlist is heavy on sherry (GlenDronach,
A'bunadh, Glenfarclas, GlenAllachie, Nàdurra) and light on wine-cask finishes — worth
correcting.

**The marmite note is the second savoury/umami note of the night**, after the Springbank's
soy sauce. Two unrelated whiskies, same register, both spotted unprompted. A working
hypothesis for this one: Chenin Blanc is often aged *sur lie* (on its spent yeast), and
Marmite is literally yeast extract — so lees-derived compounds carrying through the cask
would explain it. Unverified for these specific barrels.
